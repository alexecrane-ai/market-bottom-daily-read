Today's macro highlights

Stocks are bouncing in today's half-day Black Friday session after a rough Wednesday. Bonds are pulling the other way: hot October inflation has markets pricing a near-certain Fed hike on Dec 9, pushing the 10-year back above 4.9% and holding the dollar at its highs.

Written Fri Nov 27, 1:56 PM ET from web news and this dashboard

  • Rally, but the trends are still bearish. By late morning the S&P 500 was up about 1.4% near 7,282 and the Nasdaq about 1.9%. Tech led, Amazon rose about 4% on strong Black Friday demand, and Adobe counted about $7.1B in Thanksgiving Day online sales, up 10.4% from last year. Breadth is strong: 81.1% of stocks are up and no sector is down. Even so, SPY (728.32) and QQQ (650.99) are still in Bearish trends, sitting inside their ranges below the 741.33 and 669.6 sell levels. AMZN (254.12) is still Bearish, while TSLA turned Bullish today at 400.00, just under its 408.24 sell level.
  • The Fed is on track to hike. October core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose to 3.3% year over year against 3.2% expected, the highest in 3 years. Headline PCE came in at 3.8%. Minutes from the last Fed meeting said almost all officials think further hikes would likely be appropriate. As of Nov 26, swap-market pricing put the chance of a 0.25-point hike on Dec 9 at 96%, which would lift the 4.00% upper bound to 4.25%. Fed officials go quiet before the Dec 8–9 meeting, so the data, not Fed speeches, will move expectations next week.
  • Rates and the dollar are pushing back against the rally. The 10-year yield rose more than 6 bps to about 4.91–4.93%, in line with the dashboard's 4.91%. The 2-year is near 4.68–4.70%, and the dashboard's 10y–2y spread is +24 bps. The Dollar Index is about 104.0, which the dashboard flags as AT HIGHS. UUP (29.72) is Bullish and sits just under its 29.78 sell level, while FXE (100.98) stays Bearish. The strong dollar is one of three items still keeping the bottom checklist at 5/8.
  • Volatility has calmed but credit hasn't confirmed. Wednesday was very choppy: new tariff proposals and a sharp AI selloff took the S&P 500 down 3.2% at its intraday low before it closed 0.3% lower at 7,182.78. The VIX closed that day at 27.17 and is now 23.81, a fast drop below 25. Junk-bond spreads at 377 bps are still VOLATILE, up 51 bps in 20 days, and HYG (76.61) is Bearish between 76.08 and 76.83. The checklist also still lacks a 90% up day; the last 90% down day was Nov 19.
  • Strong growth and jobs data keep the Fed leaning toward hikes. Initial jobless claims fell to 189K, matching the lowest since 1969, and Q3 GDP is at a revised 4.3% annualized. A hot economy with inflation rising helps explain why the defensive sectors hold Bullish trends: XLU 43.73, XLP 92.68 and XLV 184.2. XLRE (44.14) is also Bullish despite a near-4.9% 10-year. Cyclical sectors XLI (165.56) and XLE (61.67) remain Bearish.
  • Commodities are mixed. Gold rose more than 1.6% to about $3,633 despite higher yields and a stronger dollar. On the dashboard, GLD (334.4) is still Bearish below its 341.4 sell level and GDX (79.18) is just under its 79.55 sell level. WTI crude was about $84.47 early, up 0.9%, but USO (137.77) has been Bearish since Nov 9.

How to read the trend signal

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Favorable upside conditions
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Improving conditions; upside potential increasing
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Weakening conditions; downside risk increasing
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ Unfavorable conditions; downside risk elevated

Near the buy level (low end of range)

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Potential buying opportunity
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Watch for support
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Caution buying dips
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ New exposure generally unfavorable

Near the sell level (top end of range)

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Potential profit-taking opportunity
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Watch for upside continuation
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Reduced or no exposure favored
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ Potential selling or shorting opportunity

The trading range marks likely turning levels; the trend signal says how to read a test of either end. They are reference levels, not guaranteed turning points. Short selling carries extra risk, including unlimited losses.

How signals are defined. Fast drop below 25: VIX closes under 25, was at or above 25 within the last 10 sessions, and is down at least 20% from its 10-day high. 90% day: at least 90% of S&P 500 members closed up (or down) versus the prior close. This is a large-cap proxy; the classic Lowry study uses NYSE up-volume and points gained. Junk spreads: ICE BofA US High Yield option-adjusted spread from FRED (usually one business day behind). Volatile = 20-day swing size in the top 20% of the past year, or widening of 30+ bps in 5 days or 50+ bps in 20 days. Calm = swing size below the 50th percentile with little widening. VIX term structure: VIX divided by the 3-month VIX; above 1 means short-term fear exceeds longer-term fear (panic), and a move back below 1 often comes near bottoms. Stocks above 50-day average: under 20% is a washout; the checklist turns on when it's above 50%, or 10+ points up from a washout low. Sectors: the 11 Select Sector SPDR funds (XLK, XLF, …). 10+ falling together = broad selling; 10+ rising = broad buying. Failed rally: Nasdaq or S&P 500 up 1%+ at its high but closing lower than the prior day. Reversal up: down 1%+ at its low but closing higher. Strongest 90% signal: a 90% up day within about 10 sessions of a 90% down day (Lowry's research). Dollar: ICE US Dollar Index. Reversing = made a 60-day high in the last 20 sessions, now 1.5%+ below it and under its 20-day average. Yield curve: 10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield from FRED (one business day behind). Flattening/steepening = 10+ bps move in 20 days; rates stable = 10-year moved less than 25 bps in 20 days. Trend ranges: buy level = 5-day exponential average minus 1.71× the 10-day average daily range (ATR); sell level = the same average plus 1.04× that range. Upside/downside = distance from today's price to the sell/buy level. Bullish/bearish = the 10-day exponential average above/below the 30-day; neutral = the price has crossed back over the 30-day against the trend (↑ in a downtrend, yellow; ↓ in an uptrend, orange). These formulas were fitted to a published buy/sell-level sheet for Nov 24, 2026: most levels within about 1%, trend direction on 40 of 41 symbols. Signal date/price = the close when the current signal began; days are calendar days. Read = near buy/sell when the price is within 25% of the range from that end. In the VIX and spread tables, red means rising (more stress) and green means falling. Not investment advice.
