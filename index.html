Today's macro highlights

Stocks extended a strong holiday-shortened week with a Black Friday tech rebound, but the macro backdrop got tighter: core PCE rose to a 3-year high of 3.3%, Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%, and jobless claims fell to 189k, so markets now see a December 9 Fed hike as nearly certain.

Written Fri Nov 27, 3:00 PM ET from web news and this dashboard

  • Inflation is moving further from the Fed's 2% goal. October core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose 3.3% y/y against a 3.2% forecast and 3.1% in September, its highest in about three years. Headline PCE was 3.8%, in line with forecasts. The dashboard's official data shows the same pattern: core CPI rose to 2.9% from 2.8% and headline CPI to 4.1% from 4.0%. After the PCE report, estimated odds of a 25 bp hike on December 9 reached 98.1%, from a target range of 3.75–4.00% (upper bound 4.00% on the dashboard).
  • Growth and jobs give the Fed little reason to wait. Q3 GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualized pace from 3.4%, the fastest in three years. Initial jobless claims fell to 189k against 200k expected, tying the 2026 low, and October payrolls were +165k. Fed officials have been hawkish: last week's minutes showed almost all officials expect more hikes, Waller said a half-point hike should be considered, and Williams said no 'dramatic' move is needed. Treasury Secretary Bessent warned that overtightening could hurt the economy. The Fed's quiet period before the meeting starts Nov 28, so no Fed speakers are expected in the coming week.
  • Stocks rose for a third straight day in the half-day session. The Dow was up about 510 points (+1%), the S&P 500 +1.2% and the Nasdaq +1.7%, led by chips, Meta and Amazon. Before today, the Dow and S&P were each up about 5% for the week, the best week since April 2025. On the dashboard, 81.1% of stocks are up, no sector is down, and the bottom checklist scores 5/8: VIX is back below 25 at 23.81 and 63% of stocks are above their 50-day averages. Still, there has been no 90% up day since the Nov 19 90% down day. SPY (728.32) and QQQ (650.99) remain in bearish trends, below their sell levels of 741.33 and 669.60.
  • Rates and the dollar are working against the rally. The 10-year yield rose about 6.5 bp to roughly 4.93% today after hitting a 2½-month low near 4.80% on Thursday; the dashboard shows 4.91%. The dollar index is near 104.00, close to a 19-month high. UUP is the only bullish index ETF on the dashboard (29.72, sell level 29.78), and its 'Dollar reversing lower' checklist item is still off. FXE (100.98) and the bearish trends in GLD (334.40), SLV and GDX are consistent with a firm dollar, although gold rose more than 1.6% today.
  • Credit has not confirmed the bounce. High-yield spreads are 377 bps and 'volatile,' up 51 bps over 20 days, and HYG (76.61) is still bearish, below its 76.83 sell level. Rate-sensitive small caps are close to a test: IWM is at 273.79, just under its 276.06 sell level, in a bearish trend since September. Despite the 4.9% 10-year, the dashboard's defensive sectors stay bullish: XLU (43.73), XLP (92.68), XLV (184.20) and XLRE (44.14). XLF and XLC turned neutral today.
  • Oil is a second inflation risk. Brent settled 3.5% higher at $94.61 on Wednesday after Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, and it was quoted at $96.33 on Nov 26. On the dashboard, energy trends have not followed: USO (137.77, sell level 142.44), XLE (61.67) and XOM, CVX, COP and EOG all remain bearish.

How to read the trend signal

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Favorable upside conditions
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Improving conditions; upside potential increasing
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Weakening conditions; downside risk increasing
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ Unfavorable conditions; downside risk elevated

Near the buy level (low end of range)

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Potential buying opportunity
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Watch for support
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Caution buying dips
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ New exposure generally unfavorable

Near the sell level (top end of range)

  • 🟢 Bullish ↑ Potential profit-taking opportunity
  • 🟡 Neutral ↑ Watch for upside continuation
  • 🟠 Neutral ↓ Reduced or no exposure favored
  • 🔴 Bearish ↓ Potential selling or shorting opportunity

The trading range marks likely turning levels; the trend signal says how to read a test of either end. They are reference levels, not guaranteed turning points. Short selling carries extra risk, including unlimited losses.

How signals are defined. Fast drop below 25: VIX closes under 25, was at or above 25 within the last 10 sessions, and is down at least 20% from its 10-day high. 90% day: at least 90% of S&P 500 members closed up (or down) versus the prior close. This is a large-cap proxy; the classic Lowry study uses NYSE up-volume and points gained. Junk spreads: ICE BofA US High Yield option-adjusted spread from FRED (usually one business day behind). Volatile = 20-day swing size in the top 20% of the past year, or widening of 30+ bps in 5 days or 50+ bps in 20 days. Calm = swing size below the 50th percentile with little widening. VIX term structure: VIX divided by the 3-month VIX; above 1 means short-term fear exceeds longer-term fear (panic), and a move back below 1 often comes near bottoms. Stocks above 50-day average: under 20% is a washout; the checklist turns on when it's above 50%, or 10+ points up from a washout low. Sectors: the 11 Select Sector SPDR funds (XLK, XLF, …). 10+ falling together = broad selling; 10+ rising = broad buying. Failed rally: Nasdaq or S&P 500 up 1%+ at its high but closing lower than the prior day. Reversal up: down 1%+ at its low but closing higher. Strongest 90% signal: a 90% up day within about 10 sessions of a 90% down day (Lowry's research). Dollar: ICE US Dollar Index. Reversing = made a 60-day high in the last 20 sessions, now 1.5%+ below it and under its 20-day average. Yield curve: 10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield from FRED (one business day behind). Flattening/steepening = 10+ bps move in 20 days; rates stable = 10-year moved less than 25 bps in 20 days. Trend ranges: buy level = 5-day exponential average minus 1.71× the 10-day average daily range (ATR); sell level = the same average plus 1.04× that range. Upside/downside = distance from today's price to the sell/buy level. Bullish/bearish = the 10-day exponential average above/below the 30-day; neutral = the price has crossed back over the 30-day against the trend (↑ in a downtrend, yellow; ↓ in an uptrend, orange). These formulas were fitted to a published buy/sell-level sheet for Nov 24, 2026: most levels within about 1%, trend direction on 40 of 41 symbols. Signal date/price = the close when the current signal began; days are calendar days. Read = near buy/sell when the price is within 25% of the range from that end. In the VIX and spread tables, red means rising (more stress) and green means falling. Not investment advice.
