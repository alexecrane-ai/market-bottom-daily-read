How signals are defined. Fast drop below 25: VIX closes under 25, was at or above 25 within the last 10 sessions, and is down at least 20% from its 10-day high. 90% day: at least 90% of S&P 500 members closed up (or down) versus the prior close. This is a large-cap proxy; the classic Lowry study uses NYSE up-volume and points gained. Junk spreads: ICE BofA US High Yield option-adjusted spread from FRED (usually one business day behind). Volatile = 20-day swing size in the top 20% of the past year, or widening of 30+ bps in 5 days or 50+ bps in 20 days. Calm = swing size below the 50th percentile with little widening. VIX term structure: VIX divided by the 3-month VIX; above 1 means short-term fear exceeds longer-term fear (panic), and a move back below 1 often comes near bottoms. Stocks above 50-day average: under 20% is a washout; the checklist turns on when it's above 50%, or 10+ points up from a washout low. Dollar: ICE US Dollar Index. Reversing = made a 60-day high in the last 20 sessions, now 1.5%+ below it and under its 20-day average. Yield curve: 10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield from FRED (one business day behind). Flattening/steepening = 10+ bps move in 20 days; rates stable = 10-year moved less than 25 bps in 20 days. In the VIX and spread tables, red means rising (more stress) and green means falling.
