Today's macro highlights

Stocks are trying to steady in a holiday-thinned Black Friday session. Wednesday's choppy trade was driven by the AI selloff and new tariff proposals from the Trump administration, and the Nasdaq Composite is now in correction territory, while this week's data shows hot inflation and strong growth.

Written Fri Nov 27, 10:00 AM ET from web news and this dashboard

  • Wednesday before Thanksgiving was mixed and very volatile. The Dow rose 0.3% to 50,182.95 after falling as much as 2.2% intraday, the S&P 500 slipped 0.25%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.6% to 23,585.80 after being down as much as 4.6%. The Nasdaq is now 14.2% below its all-time high, which puts it in correction territory. The causes were new tariffs proposed by the Trump administration and a sharp drop in AI stocks. Mixed economic data helped markets win back part of the losses.
  • Tech is still where the damage is. QQQ (637.70) and XLK (156.53) have both been in Bearish trends since Oct 19. QQQ is about halfway between its buy level of 597.17 and its sell level of 664.60. Chip stocks bounced late Wednesday (MU +5.65%, NVDA +2.75%), but both remain in Bearish trends: MU at 612.74 against a 707.83 sell level, and NVDA at 146.03 against 165.60.
  • The inflation data points toward tighter policy. October core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose to 3.3% from 3.1%, headline PCE rose to 3.8%, and CPI is at 4.1%. Meanwhile Q3 GDP was revised to 4.3% annualized and initial jobless claims fell to 189k. Strong growth, a tight labor market and faster inflation make it hard for the Fed to ease after it raised its target to 4.00% in September. That fits the 10-year yield at 4.91% and the dollar at its highs (DXY 103.9, with UUP Bullish at 29.68, just under its 29.76 sell level).
  • Stress gauges have not cleared yet. The market-bottom checklist scores only 2/8. VIX is 26.89 (it closed Wednesday at 27.17), the VIX term structure is still inverted at 1.05, and there has been no 90% up day since the Nov 19 90% down day. Junk spreads have widened 54 bps in 20 days to 372 bps, and HYG (76.57) is in a Bearish trend just below its 76.82 sell level.
  • Investors are clearly favoring defensive stocks. XLV, XLP, XLU and XLRE are all in Bullish trends, as are staples names like PG, COST, WMT and KO. Cyclicals and commodities are Bearish: XLI, XLE, USO at 139.69, CPER and GSG. Breadth is improving: 62% of stocks are above their 50-day averages (up from a 20-day low of 43%), 71.9% of stocks are up today, and XLF and XLC moved from Bearish to Neutral today.
  • The 10-year yield is down 9 bps over 20 days and the 2s10s curve is +24 bps. That relief on rates is one of the two checklist signals that are on, and it helps explain why rate-sensitive XLU and XLRE are holding up. A further jump in inflation expectations would put that support at risk. How signals are defined. Fast drop below 25: VIX closes under 25, was at or above 25 within the last 10 sessions, and is down at least 20% from its 10-day high. 90% day: at least 90% of S&P 500 members closed up (or down) versus the prior close. This is a large-cap proxy; the classic Lowry study uses NYSE up-volume and points gained. Junk spreads: ICE BofA US High Yield option-adjusted spread from FRED (usually one business day behind). Volatile = 20-day swing size in the top 20% of the past year, or widening of 30+ bps in 5 days or 50+ bps in 20 days. Calm = swing size below the 50th percentile with little widening. VIX term structure: VIX divided by the 3-month VIX; above 1 means short-term fear exceeds longer-term fear (panic), and a move back below 1 often comes near bottoms. Stocks above 50-day average: under 20% is a washout; the checklist turns on when it's above 50%, or 10+ points up from a washout low. Sectors: the 11 Select Sector SPDR funds (XLK, XLF, …). 10+ falling together = broad selling; 10+ rising = broad buying. Failed rally: Nasdaq or S&P 500 up 1%+ at its high but closing lower than the prior day. Reversal up: down 1%+ at its low but closing higher. Strongest 90% signal: a 90% up day within about 10 sessions of a 90% down day (Lowry's research). Dollar: ICE US Dollar Index. Reversing = made a 60-day high in the last 20 sessions, now 1.5%+ below it and under its 20-day average. Yield curve: 10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield from FRED (one business day behind). Flattening/steepening = 10+ bps move in 20 days; rates stable = 10-year moved less than 25 bps in 20 days. Trend ranges: buy level = 5-day exponential average minus 1.71× the 10-day average daily range (ATR); sell level = the same average plus 1.04× that range. Upside/downside = distance from today's price to the sell/buy level. Bullish/bearish = the 10-day exponential average above/below the 30-day; neutral = the price has crossed back over the 30-day against the trend (↑ in a downtrend, yellow; ↓ in an uptrend, orange). These formulas were fitted to a published buy/sell-level sheet for Nov 24, 2026: most levels within about 1%, trend direction on 40 of 41 symbols. Signal date/price = the close when the current signal began; days are calendar days. Read = near buy/sell when the price is within 25% of the range from that end. In the VIX and spread tables, red means rising (more stress) and green means falling. Not investment advice.
